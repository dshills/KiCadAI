This is an electrical-interface board; application firmware is out of scope. Use catalog-qualified parts and provide an accessible wired programming interface, defined reset/boot behavior, and justified power decoupling. Operate from -10 to 50 degrees C ambient. Fit inside a 100 by 80 mm rectangular enclosure, with all user connectors reachable from the perimeter; at most four copper layers are permitted. Supply and signal return connections must be accessible. Rail voltage windows apply after startup settles while the declared supply is present; separately stated startup, reset, reverse-input and powered-off safety limits still apply. Demonstrate supply/load/tolerance/temperature corners, startup behavior, and applicable junction-temperature limits. The schematic must identify the functional groups, interfaces and supplies clearly. Deliver a fully routed native KiCad project passing real ERC, strict DRC, writer and normalized round-trip checks, with repeatable downstream evidence. Do not provide firmware or claim manufactured-hardware validation.

Create a programmable controller board powered directly by a current-limited 4.75 to 5.25 V source, with no more than 100 mA total input. It must exchange 5 V UART signals at 115200 baud and operate an external 3.3 V SPI peripheral at up to 1 MHz using clock, controller-to-peripheral data, peripheral-to-controller data and one chip select. Provide a 3.3 V auxiliary output within 3.15 to 3.45 V delivering up to 20 mA to that peripheral. UART and SPI line capacitance is at most 20 pF per line; external traces are at most 50 mm. The SPI peripheral presents CMOS inputs and drives a CMOS data output in the 3.3 V domain; signals must not overvoltage it. Chip select must be inactive high during reset. The UART host and peripheral are powered whenever the controller is powered, and never drive an unpowered board. Firmware protocol conversion is not requested; prove only the powered electrical interfaces and reset behavior.
